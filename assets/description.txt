This haiku suggests a quiet acceptance of loss or change. The phrase "Let it fall rather" implies that it is better to allow something to happen naturally than to force control over it. "Nor are those empty hearted" hints that true strength does not come from being emotionally detached, but from having a sincere and open heart. The final line, "reserue thy state," appears to mean preserving one’s dignity, inner peace, or rightful condition. Overall, the poem reflects a calm, philosophical attitude: it values restraint, emotional honesty, and the wisdom of not clinging too tightly to what must pass.
