This haiku suggests a dark and unsettling scene in which someone dreams that a wolf can be calmed or persuaded by the right words. The phrase "with certain words appeaseth" implies a hopeful belief that language can tame danger, but the final line shatters that hope: the wolf "mangleth all his wife." The poem creates a grim contrast between trust in speech and the violent reality that follows. Read as a symbolic piece, the wolf may represent cruelty, instinct, or betrayal, while the wife’s fate shows the failure of reason or persuasion to stop destruction. The archaic language gives the haiku a solemn, almost biblical tone, making the violence feel even more tragic and fated.
