This haiku captures a quiet moment on the water just before nature turns restless. The phrase "set by a current" suggests something being carried gently along, while "fast sleeping on the water" creates a calm, still image that feels almost dreamlike. However, the final line, "with a rising gale," introduces tension and change, hinting that the peaceful scene will soon be disturbed by stronger winds. The poem contrasts stillness with movement, calm with danger, and serenity with the power of nature.
