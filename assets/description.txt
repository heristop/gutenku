This haiku creates a quiet, slightly mysterious scene in just a few words. The image of the clock striking three suggests a late hour, when most people are asleep or absent. The phrase "the gentlemen being out" hints that the men are not present, perhaps away from home or engaged elsewhere, and "as might be supposed" adds a tone of dry, understated observation. Together, the lines suggest stillness, emptiness, and a subtle sense of social distance or secrecy. The poem’s power lies in its restraint: it says very little directly, yet invites us to imagine what is happening beyond the scene.
