This haiku suggests a calm, reflective state of mind. The opening line, "If he thought at all," implies that the speaker is uncertain whether the person being described is truly reflective or even fully aware. The next line, "Then everything was right," presents the possibility that thought itself brings order, harmony, or correctness to the world. However, the final line, "On the other hand," suddenly shifts the tone, introducing doubt, contradiction, and the idea that another perspective may exist. As a whole, the haiku explores uncertainty, balance, and the fragile nature of judgment, leaving the reader suspended between certainty and contradiction.
