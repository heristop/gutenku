This haiku creates a playful and surprising image by placing a highly intelligent and scientific figure — “an able and learned chemist” — beside the delicate, almost impossible idea of “a silken ladder.” The phrase “on the other hand” suggests contrast, as if the poem is weighing two different possibilities or truths. The chemist represents reason, knowledge, and precision, while the silken ladder feels fragile, elegant, and dreamlike. Together, they form a striking juxtaposition that invites the reader to imagine intellect reaching for something soft, strange, or unreachable. The poem’s humor and mystery come from this unexpected combination, leaving the meaning open and evocative.
