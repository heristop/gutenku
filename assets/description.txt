This haiku feels like a brief, intimate moment of observation, where the speaker compares two figures side by side. The first line, "I was still staring," suggests surprise, fascination, or quiet reflection, as if the speaker cannot look away. The next line, "the other was tall and fair," gives a clear physical impression of one person, evoking elegance, brightness, or beauty. The final line, "One was a little," creates a sense of incompleteness or fragmentation, making the poem feel intentionally open-ended. As a haiku, it captures a fleeting visual memory and leaves the reader wondering about the relationship between the two figures and what exactly is being contrasted. Its power lies in its simplicity, ambiguity, and the emotional space between the lines.
