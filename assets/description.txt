This haiku creates a quiet, mysterious scene that feels like the transition from day into night. The opening line, "The lamps had been lit," suggests evening has arrived and the world is settling into darkness. The second line, "got into my walking clothes," adds a personal, intimate touch, as if the speaker is preparing to go out into the night or begin a reflective journey. The final line, "plunging in his hand," is more abrupt and ambiguous, which gives the poem a dreamlike quality. It may suggest a sudden physical action, or it may symbolize being drawn into something deeper, perhaps emotion, memory, or another person's presence. Overall, the haiku feels atmospheric and slightly surreal, leaving the reader with a sense of movement, transition, and uncertainty.
