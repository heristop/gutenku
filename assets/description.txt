This haiku suggests that true generosity does not depend on having many people around us. Instead, it celebrates a generous spirit that finds joy in giving, in kindness, and in appreciating even a single person or moment. The phrase "no need of persons" implies a sense of independence from crowd or company, while "generous spirit is most" highlights the value of an open, giving heart. The final line, "take pleasure in one," can be read as finding delight in one person, one act of kindness, or one simple thing. Overall, the poem reflects quiet contentment and the idea that generosity is a state of mind, not a social display.
