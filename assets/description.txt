This haiku captures a strange, uneasy calm at cocktail hour, a time usually associated with social ease and polished conversation. The word "exiles" suggests people who are displaced, isolated, or emotionally cut off, yet in this moment they "become peaceful," as if the ritual of drinking and gathering briefly softens their pain. The final phrase, "are all the bloodless," is especially striking: it can imply emotional numbness, exhaustion, or a loss of vitality. The poem therefore creates a mood of muted tension, where outward calm seems to hide inner emptiness. Its power lies in the contrast between social refinement and the haunting suggestion of detachment or lifelessness.
