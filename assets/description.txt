This haiku reflects on the nursery-school as a place of early guidance and shaping. The speaker seems to question how young children should be taught or managed in order to achieve a desired outcome, suggesting concern with education, discipline, or moral development. The phrase "What is to be done with them" gives the poem a thoughtful, almost anxious tone, as if the speaker is considering the responsibility of adults toward children. Overall, the haiku captures the tension between innocence and the need to prepare children for a specific future.
