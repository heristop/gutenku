This haiku evokes a moment of deep emotional pain and quiet accusation. The phrase "with sorrow of heart" suggests inner grief, while "Her eyen were black as sloe" compares her eyes to dark sloe berries, creating a vivid image of sadness, mystery, or intensity. The final address, "thou proud baron," introduces a tone of reproach, as if the speaker is condemning a nobleman whose pride may have caused her suffering. Together, the lines blend beauty, sorrow, and social tension into a compact but powerful emotional scene.
