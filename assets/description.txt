This haiku suggests a quiet, outwardly orderly moment that hides inner effort. The phrase "things were right enough" implies a sense of acceptable calm or stability, but not perfect happiness. "The silence was unbroken" creates an atmosphere of stillness, restraint, and perhaps emotional distance. The final line, "exerting herself," introduces tension: beneath the calm surface, someone is working hard, struggling, or forcing themselves to continue. The poem’s power lies in this contrast between external quiet and internal effort, leaving the reader to sense hidden pressure or endurance.
