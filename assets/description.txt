This haiku feels like a plea for guidance in a moment of confusion or conflict. The speaker addresses the “right hand,” which can suggest strength, loyalty, or an inner helper, and asks it to explain how things have become so quiet or restrained. The phrase “are put to silence” creates a mood of tension, as if voices, actions, or truths have been suppressed. The language is compact but dramatic, leaving the reader with a sense of urgency, uncertainty, and hidden emotion.
