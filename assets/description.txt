This haiku creates a quiet, haunting scene in which the speaker watches flames brighten a room, suggesting both literal light and a moment of tense awareness. The phrase "watching each blaze up" gives the image movement and intensity, as if the fire is growing one flare at a time. By the second line, "The room was light enough now," the setting feels illuminated, but the word "enough" suggests a limited, uneasy comfort rather than warmth or peace. The final line, "His wife did not write," shifts the poem from physical light to emotional absence. The lack of a letter implies silence, distance, or perhaps grief, making the brightness of the room feel ironically hollow. Overall, the haiku uses simple imagery to convey longing, disappointment, and the painful weight of unreturned communication.
