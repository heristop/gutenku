This haiku presents a striking contrast between size and innocence. The first line, "large though it was," suggests something immense, perhaps a school, institution, or community. Yet the next lines reveal that it was "composed wholly of boys," showing that its entire population is made up of children. The final phrase, "Its population," feels almost like a documentary or report, giving the poem a factual tone. Together, the lines create a picture of a large, boy-filled world, and they hint at youthful energy, discipline, and the scale of boyhood life within a shared space.
