This haiku captures the tense, almost absurd experience of dealing with debt and persistent reminders through the phrase "dunning letters." The opening line, "which took five minutes," suggests something brief or small, but the next line reveals a heavier reality: the speaker is being harassed by collection notices. The final line, "what a way to talk," adds irony and dry humor, as if the letters themselves are speaking in a cold, impersonal voice. Overall, the poem highlights the clash between ordinary time and emotional pressure, using concise language to show frustration, embarrassment, and wit.
