This haiku feels dreamlike and elusive, using unusual phrasing to create a sense of mystery and reflection. The opening line, "Were I but so light," suggests a wish for freedom, delicacy, or release from burden. The second line, "where like conference is held," introduces an abstract, almost ceremonial space—perhaps a place of meeting, exchange, or inner dialogue. The final line, "The horse remember," gives the poem a striking, symbolic ending: the horse may represent strength, instinct, or memory itself, as if even something powerful and physical is capable of recalling the past. Overall, the haiku invites readers to think about memory, weight, and the contrast between human longing and animal instinct, while its fragmented language enhances its mysterious, contemplative mood.
