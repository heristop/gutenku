This haiku creates a peaceful, almost dreamlike scene in which a man’s dog and pipe are both resting on the grass. The phrase "slept" gives the image of stillness and calm, while "As for the greatest number" suggests that many others may also be asleep or at rest, expanding the quiet mood beyond just these two objects. The final line, "on the grass asleep," reinforces the softness of the setting and makes nature feel like a place of universal peace. Overall, the haiku captures a moment of deep tranquility, where even ordinary objects seem to share in the stillness of the world.
